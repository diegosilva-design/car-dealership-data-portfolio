# How I work

A 4-step method I apply to any process: sales, service shop, trade-in appraisals or deliveries.

## 1 · Diagnosis: understand how work happens today
- Short interviews with the people who run the process, not just the managers.
- On-site observation: what people *do*, which isn't always what they *say* they do.
- Export the data that already exists, even if it's incomplete.
- **Deliverable:** a map of the current process (AS-IS) and findings backed by numbers.

## 2 · Detection: find where time and money are lost
- Steps with no owner, waiting, rework, information lost between teams.
- Root cause with the "5 whys": the focus is on the process, not on blaming people.
- **Deliverable:** what the problem costs, in hours and in money.

## 3 · Redesign: propose a better, simpler workflow
- Use the tools the company already has first.
- Automate the repetitive work (assigning, notifying, reminding) and leave judgment calls to people.
- Define owners, deadlines (SLAs) and KPIs before implementing anything.
- **Deliverable:** a map of the proposed process (TO-BE), a pilot plan and risks.

## 4 · Measurement: prove it worked
- Forms that capture clean data at the source.
- A dashboard comparing the baseline against the pilot.
- Economic impact in scenarios (conservative, base, optimistic), with no inflated promises.
- **Deliverable:** a dashboard and a pilot evaluation with a recommendation.

---

## Express diagnosis: 10 questions to map a process in 30 minutes

1. How does the process start? What triggers it?
2. Where does the information come in? How many different channels are there?
3. Who decides which person takes ownership? How do they decide?
4. Where is each step recorded? (CRM, spreadsheet, notebook, text messages, memory)
5. How long should each stage take, and how long does it actually take?
6. What happens when something falls behind? Who finds out?
7. At which step are the most cases lost, or the most complaints raised?
8. Which repetitive task takes up the most of the team's time?
9. What number do you look at today to know if things are going well? How often?
10. If you could change just one thing about the process tomorrow, what would it be?
