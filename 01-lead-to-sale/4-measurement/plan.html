The quarter at a glance i Both ordering plans are run against the same expected demand for the next 3 months, starting from today's stock. Current rule: last year's sales for the same months +5%. Forecast plan: enough to end each month with 50 days of expected sales plus a safety buffer. Cost = cars sitting on the lot + cars borrowed from sister stores + gross profit lost when customers buy elsewhere. Change the assumptions below to test the conclusion.

Recommended orders i What to order each month under the forecast plan, next to what the current rule would order. Days of supply = cars in stock ÷ expected daily sales; healthy is 45–60. Action summarizes the change against today's rule. Click a column title to sort.

Where each plan leaves the lot i Days of supply at the end of the quarter, by segment. The shaded band is the healthy 45–60 days. The current rule leaves growing segments nearly empty and sedans far above the band; the forecast plan lands every segment inside it.

What each plan costs i Total cost over the quarter, split into holding (cars on the lot), transfers (cars borrowed from sister stores) and lost gross profit (customers who bought elsewhere). Running a thin lot looks cheap on holding cost, but the lost sales cost much more. Updates with the assumptions below.
