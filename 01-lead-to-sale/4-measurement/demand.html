Measure demand by
Segment mix · last 12 months i Each card shows a segment's share of demand over the last 12 months (L12M) and how it changed against the 12 months before. Leads measure customer interest; sales measure what was actually sold, which also depends on stock and on the sales team (the pilot from Jul 2026 raised sales in every segment).

Segment mix over time i Each segment's share of all leads (or sales) per quarter. A line going up means customers are moving toward that segment, regardless of how busy the store was overall. Hover to see every segment for a quarter.

Seasonality i How busy each month is for each segment, compared with that segment's average month (100 = average). Blue cells are above average, red cells below. Long-term growth or decline is removed first (each month is compared with its 12-month moving average), so a fast-growing segment does not look "busier" just because it is growing. Hover a cell for the details.

Affordability i Share of leads that ended in a sale, for vehicles up to $35K and over $35K, by year. When financing gets more expensive, the gap between the two widens: buyers still look at expensive vehicles, but fewer of them close.

Leads as an early signal i Leads and sales for one segment, each as a 3-month rolling average indexed to 100 = the average month before the pilot (Jan 2024 – Jun 2026), so both fit on one scale. Leads show interest first and are not affected by changes in the sales process; sales follow a few weeks later and jump with the Jul 2026 pilot. That is why the forecast (next step) predicts leads, then converts them into sales.

Demand by area i Each row shows how one area's demand splits across segments over the last 12 months (rows add up to 100%). Blue means the segment is more popular there than across DFW as a whole; red means less. Use it to decide where to advertise each segment. The Area filter does not apply to this table.

Inventory health i Days of supply = cars in stock ÷ average daily sales over the last 3 months. A healthy lot holds 45–60 days (shaded band). Below that, customers find nothing to test drive and cars must be borrowed from sister stores (transfers, which cost money); above it, cars sit and lose value. Inventory is store-wide, so the Area filter does not apply.
